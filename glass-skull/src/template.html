Frosted glass
How the light is traced

Each pixel follows light through the glass. It refracts at the front surface with Snell’s law, travels through the skull’s real thickness (measured from a back-face depth pass), and refracts again on the way out, or reflects internally when the angle is past the critical angle. Then it hits the backdrop plane. Red, green and blue use different indices from the Abbe number, so edges split into colour. Fresnel sets how much is reflected at each surface. Absorption and scattering grow with the path length inside. Frost is modelled as a sandblasted surface: micro-facet normals scatter each ray into a cone, so the backdrop blurs more the farther it is from the glass.

Blender material

Select your mesh in Blender 4.2+, open Scripting, paste and run. Cycles path-traces the same glass (rough transmission, dispersion, thin-film) and the script can add a grey backdrop with large black letters behind the object for it to refract.
